# UX220-Lab-1
My favourite thing that I have written is the TD case study in my UX portfolio. It describes the solution I created to make TD EasyWeb even Easier for users. This was the first UX case study that I created completely outside of work and school. I worked on it independently over the summer break because I wanted to create something that reflected my own interests and experiences.

It was my favourite because I got to take my experience working at TD and turn it into something creative and meaningful. I put a lot of thought, ideas, research, and personal experience into the case study. I had coffee chats with people working in different areas of the industry, learned from their experiences, researched how people interact with digital banking, and thought about problems I had noticed through working at TD. I noticed that users had problems locating areas for language preferences, GIC information, and how to contact TD regarding credit cards. I noticed that a lot of users who had the App would still come into the branch to ask how to find or manage credit card information even though the information was already on the App. This meant that the information was not easily accessible. I came up with a solution for a feature that makes information like that more accessible. 

What makes this case study especially important to me is the amount of effort I put into it and a visual representation of what I learned from my experience working at TD. It gave me something meaningful to add to my portfolio that reflects my growth, skills, and experience, and that I am proud to share with others. After completing it, I had a coffee chat with someone who had experience working at IBM, and I showed them my portfolio and case study. It was well received, which made me even more proud of what I had created. It felt rewarding to have someone with industry experience recognize the work I had put into it.

This is the first piece of work that I feel truly proud of because it is personal to me. It combines my experience working at TD with what I have learned in UX design, while also showing the skills I developed on my own. More than just an assignment, it represents my growth as a UX designer and reminds me that I can take my own experiences and turn them into something valuable.
